ADHD Prescribing in Context

ADHD medication prescribing in Northern Ireland sits within a wider landscape of mental health need that is strongly linked to deprivation. Data from the Department of Health's Health Inequalities Annual Report 2025 shows that mood and anxiety prescription rates in the most deprived areas of Northern Ireland are around two-thirds higher than in the least deprived areas — a gap that has remained persistent over time.

Self-harm admissions and suicide rates follow a similar pattern, with the most deprived communities consistently experiencing the highest rates. These are the same communities where demand for all mental health services, including ADHD diagnosis and treatment, is greatest.

Research internationally has found that around 25% of prisoners meet diagnostic criteria for ADHD — a five to tenfold increase compared to the general population (Young et al, Psychological Medicine, 2015). Untreated ADHD is associated with higher rates of substance misuse, self-harm, and contact with the criminal justice system. Early diagnosis and appropriate treatment in primary care is one of the most effective interventions available.

The prescribing data on this site cannot tell us whether ADHD is being over- or under-diagnosed in any area. What it can show is where medication is being prescribed, how that has changed over a decade, and how individual practices compare to their peers. We hope this transparency supports better informed conversations about ADHD care in Northern Ireland.
