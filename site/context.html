Why This Matters for ADHD

ADHD does not exist in isolation. It sits within a broader landscape of mental health need that, in Northern Ireland, is among the most acute in the UK. The indicators on this page — mood and anxiety prescribing, self-harm admissions, and suicide rates — are not separate issues. For many people they are interconnected, and ADHD is frequently at the centre of that connection.

Research consistently shows that undiagnosed or untreated ADHD is associated with significantly elevated risk of substance misuse, self-harm, depression, anxiety, and contact with the criminal justice system. A landmark study found that around 25% of the prison population meet diagnostic criteria for ADHD — a figure that speaks directly to what happens when the condition goes unrecognised through childhood and into adulthood. (Young et al., Psychological Medicine, 2015)

Early diagnosis and appropriate prescribing in primary care is one of the most cost-effective interventions available. The variation in ADHD prescribing rates across NI's Health Trusts — visible throughout this site — reflects unequal access to assessment and treatment, not differences in underlying need.

The deprivation gradients visible in every indicator on this page are stark. The most deprived communities consistently show higher rates of mood and anxiety prescribing, self-harm admissions, and suicide — and those same communities are often the least well-served by specialist ADHD services. This page is an attempt to make that connection visible.

ADHD prescribing rate GP Prescribing Data, Business Services Organisation (BSO), via OpenDataNI Items prescribed per 1,000 registered GP patients. This denominator differs from the population-based denominators used in HSCIMS indicators — direct numerical comparison should be made with caution.

Denominator note: ADHD rates on this page are calculated from items prescribed per 1,000 registered GP patients. All other mental health indicators use persons prescribed or events per 1,000 general population. These denominators differ slightly and direct numerical comparison should be made with caution.
